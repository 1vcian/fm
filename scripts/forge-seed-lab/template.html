Ascension and the November data update

The November 16 tables add Heroic, Infinite and Eternal ages. They require forge ascension 3 and the new forge levels above 35. Ascensions 1 and 2 keep the level-35 cap; ascension 3 permits level 50.

After any forge ascension, all item ages use two secondary bonuses. Ascension resets hidden item-level counters. Its equipment damage/health multiplier is ×50, ×2,500 or ×125,000; these multipliers do not stack across ascension levels and do not multiply item-sale coins.

Use the correct data version and forge ascension before entering results. Do not mix observations across ascensions, data changes or forge levels. Counts, gates and multipliers follow the Helper data and code; independent ascended game captures remain a validation requirement.

How full-domain recovery works

Each rounded percentage bounds a raw 32-bit PCG output. The solver enumerates inverse output/rotation hypotheses, which constrain the upper 37 bits of an internal RNG state. Because forge seeds advance by one, outputs at the same draw position have a known affine relationship. The solver intersects the resulting carry constraints, then resolves the remaining lower 27 bits against other stats, age and slot draws.

Every surviving seed is checked against the complete visible sequence, including possible item rotations. No seed range or ground-truth seed is supplied. Large state sets or coarse measurements can hit the explicit work budget; this returns incomplete, not a false unique answer.

The included examples are generated regression data. Recovering their seeds checks inversion of the bundled model. Actual in-game item displays still need independent validation of rounding and configuration.

How to enter percentages and interpret recovery

If the item says 11.4%, enter 11.4. If it says 4.43%, enter 4.43. Enter all decimals shown. The matcher allows half a unit in the last entered decimal place, plus a tiny fixed-point tolerance. Both stat rows can be entered in either order. A cooldown reduction can be entered with or without its minus sign. Leave a stat unselected if you don’t know it.

Item names help constrain possible item rotations. The tool keeps multiple starting rotations when the evidence does not distinguish them. Displayed item levels provide evidence even when an item has no bonuses. Primary health/damage are not separate inputs. Level-based recovery infers compatible hidden progression and equipment caps.

Tick Unknown roll if a forge happened but you did not record its result. It still advances the seed by one.

After a short typing pause, the search runs again when you change your inputs. You can keep adding rolls while it searches. Automatic searches begin after three complete known rolls. Plan to enter at least ten consecutive results: some sequences resolve sooner, while others need more than ten. Unknown entries preserve roll order but do not count as known evidence. The evidence bar measures readiness, not accuracy.

Use consecutive forges from one ascension from one forge level. Different game tables or modified item-stat displays can invalidate a match.

No hidden seed, GUID or snapshot is used. Complete bonus percentages on several items give the inverse calculation more information. Keep the rolls in order and include Unknown roll entries for missing forges.
